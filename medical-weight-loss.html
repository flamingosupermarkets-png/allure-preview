The short answer

Allure offers provider-supervised medical weight loss in Plantation, FL. A board-certified nurse practitioner reviews your history and labs, prescribes semaglutide or tirzepatide if it's safe for you, and checks in monthly. Nutrition coaching and InBody body-composition scans are included. Programs run a 3-month minimum.

Your first three months

What happens, and when. Three-month minimum, then month to month.

  1. Week 1

    Consult, labs and InBody scan

    Alicia reviews your history and baseline labs. Vanessa measures body composition so we track fat and muscle, not just the scale.

  2. Weeks 2–4

    Start low and adjust

    Your first dose, nutrition plan and a check-in on side effects. We only increase when you're ready.

  3. Month 2

    Check-in and second InBody

    Dose review with Alicia, coaching with Vanessa on protein, strength and habits that protect muscle.

  4. Month 3 and on

    Plan for the long term

    Monthly visits until you reach your goal, then a plan to step down medication and keep the results.

What the research shows

14.9%average body-weight loss with semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly over 68 weeks, vs. 2.4% with placebo1
20.9%average body-weight loss with tirzepatide 15 mg weekly over 72 weeks, vs. 3.1% with placebo2

Both studies paired medication with diet and exercise and used brand-name medication. Individual results vary, and these are not results promised by Allure.

1 Wilding JPH et al., STEP 1, New England Journal of Medicine, 2021. 2 Jastreboff AM et al., SURMOUNT-1, New England Journal of Medicine, 2022.

Safety first

These medications aren't right for everyone. You should not take them if you or your family have a history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN2, if you're pregnant, breastfeeding or planning a pregnancy, or if you've had pancreatitis. Tell us about all your medications, including insulin.

Common side effects include nausea, constipation or diarrhea, and fatigue, mostly as the dose increases.

About compounded medication: if Allure uses compounded versions compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide are not FDA-approved and are prepared by a licensed pharmacy.

Frequently asked

Your provider will go over anything specific to you at your consultation.

Semaglutide or tirzepatide: which is better?

Neither is better for everyone. Semaglutide is often the starting point; tirzepatide acts on two hormone receptors and may suit people who have plateaued. Your provider recommends one based on your history and labs.

Am I a candidate?

Most programs are for adults with a BMI of 30, or 27 with a weight-related condition. Some medical histories rule these medications out, which is why we do a consultation and labs first.

What side effects are common?

Mild nausea, digestive changes and fatigue are the most common, especially when the dose increases. We start low and adjust at each check-in.

How much weight will I lose?

It varies with your starting point, dose and consistency. We don't promise a number; we track your progress monthly and adjust the plan.

Is it medically supervised?

Yes. A licensed provider evaluates and monitors every patient for the full program.
